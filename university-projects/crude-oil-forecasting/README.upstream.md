# Time-Series-Final-Project

Crude oil is one of the most actively traded commodities in the world. We analyze daily crude oil price data from the U.S. Energy Information Administration, covering both WTI and Brent benchmarks over roughly 9,700 observations spanning more than 35 years. The two primary global benchmarks are WTI and Brent, which are standardized crude oil contracts used as pricing references for oil markets worldwide. WTI is the main benchmark in North America, while Brent is the primary benchmark for Europe and much of the global market.

Because oil markets are globally integrated, price movements in these benchmarks influence equities, currencies, inflation, and energy markets across the world.  

Our project focuses on three main research questions.

First, can classical time-series models accurately forecast crude oil prices and returns over a 63-day horizon, which roughly corresponds to one business quarter?

Second, can GARCH models capture the volatility clustering that is clearly visible in oil return data?

And third, how much did major geopolitical shocks move crude oil prices, measured in dollars per barrel?
